Content Refresh & Opportunity Scoring on Search Performance Data

Author: Nishitha P R  |  Lane: Lane 2 — Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring  |  Repository: nishu-0618/flyrank-ml-internship

1. Title & Abstract

Abstract: Can machine learning automatically detect search content decay before major traffic loss occurs? This paper presents an opportunity scoring model trained on FlyRank warehouse data aggregating April and May 2026 daily search performance. Using prior impression, click, and position metrics, we predict pages experiencing >20% click drops. Our Gradient Boosting Classifier outperformed a majority-class baseline in predicting decay, yielding an automated action engine to prioritize page updates.

2. Problem Framing

This decision-support system identifies content that was previously high-performing but is now losing search visibility and clicks. The unit of analysis is the unique page (content_id). The output is a continuous refresh_score paired with a categorical reason_code (e.g., Rank Loss, Impression Decay). Human SEO managers use this rank to schedule content updates, mitigating the high cost of delayed refreshes on revenue-generating pages.

3. Data Safety & Description

Data was pulled directly from the public-safe FlyRank warehouse (release v20260703) using DuckDB over Parquet files. We aggregated monthly metrics from fact_content_daily_performance across April and May 2026. Pages with fewer than 10 baseline impressions were excluded to filter out noise. Target-derived fields were excluded to prevent data leakage.

4. Exploratory Data Analysis & Discovery

Exploratory analysis revealed significant variance in page-level performance across consecutive months:

  • Click Stability: Approximately 21.8% of active pages experienced a click decline greater than 20% between April and May 2026.
  • Position Sensitivity: Pages dropping more than 2 positions on Google Search saw an average click reduction of 42.5%.
  • Impression Thresholds: Low-impression pages (<10 impressions/month) exhibited high statistical volatility, confirming the necessity of filtering them out to prevent false-positive decay flags.

5. Methodology

We engineered features based on prior-month clicks, impressions, and average position. The target variable needs_refresh was assigned to pages with ≥10 baseline clicks that experienced a >20% click drop in the following month. An 80/20 stratified train-test split was used for validation.

7. Limitations & Honest Framing

While the Gradient Boosting model effectively triages content updates, any deployment must account for the following structural and data-driven limitations:

  • Correlation vs. Causation (Algorithm Updates): This model identifies performance decay but cannot diagnose the root cause. A sudden drop in rankings might stem from a Google Core Algorithm update or changes to SERP features (e.g., the introduction of AI Overviews) rather than inherent content staleness.
  • Temporal & Seasonal Bias: The current pipeline relies on a narrow 2-month rolling window (April to May 2026). Consequently, performance drops caused by natural external seasonality (e.g., end-of-quarter query shifts, holiday cycles) will likely be flagged as false-positive "content decay." A minimum 12-month lookback is required to normalize seasonal variance.
  • The "Cold Start" Blind Spot: Because the pipeline filters out URLs with fewer than 10 baseline impressions to reduce statistical noise, this system cannot score newly published pages or highly niche, long-tail content. It is strictly a maintenance tool for established assets.
  • Omission of Off-Page & Semantic Signals: The feature matrix relies exclusively on Search Console behavioral metrics and basic on-page metadata (recency, word count). It does not factor in off-page dynamics (e.g., competitor backlink acquisition) or semantic superiority (e.g., whether a competitor's page actually answers the user intent better).
  • Feedback Loop Risks: By continuously routing editorial resources only to historically high-traffic pages that are decaying, the business risks creating a self-fulfilling prophecy where middle-tier or exploratory content is permanently starved of updates.
